Senior cat care

Senior Cat
Care.

Good senior cat care is mostly small routines done consistently: easy access to food, water and the litter box, gentle grooming, a warm place to rest, and regular vet checks. This guide gives you a daily, monthly and twice-yearly routine, and links to a detailed page on each part of caring for an older cat at home.

Vets count a cat as a senior after 10 (see when a cat is considered senior). For medical problems and warning signs, see our senior cat health guide. This page is about daily life.

Daily care

  • Litter box: VCA advises scooping at least once a day. Check the box when you scoop: changes in volume or stool are early clues.
  • Food and water: keep bowls easy to reach, and feed at consistent times. Routine helps older cats feel secure.
  • Play and attention: short sessions, puzzle feeders and gentle games keep the mind active.
  • Watch for change: note drinking, appetite, movement and mood. The American Association of Feline Practitioners says any change in a cat's normal behaviour or routine can be a sign of pain.

Monthly and yearly care

  • Weigh your cat and write it down. Our healthy cat weight guide explains what to look for.
  • Trim nails about monthly, since claws thicken with age.
  • See the vet at least every six months once your cat is over 10. The AAFP suggests more often after 15. Find a clinic with our cat vet finder.
  • Ask about teeth. Dental pain is common and often hidden; see cat dental disease.

When to call the vet

Breathing difficulty, collapse, straining in the litter box with little or no urine, not eating at all, and repeated vomiting need urgent help. For the full list by urgency, see the when to call the vet table.

Common conditions to know about

Care at home works alongside medical care. Read more about kidney disease, hyperthyroidism and arthritis, three of the most common problems in older cats. To see how old your cat is in human years, use the PurrAge calculator, or read every cat life stage.

Frequently asked questions

How do I take care of a senior cat?

Keep a steady routine, make the litter box, food and bed easy to reach, brush gently, trim nails, watch for changes, and see the vet at least every six months.

How often should a senior cat see the vet?

Guidelines recommend every six months for cats over 10, with the AAFP suggesting more often after 15.

What do older cats need most?

Comfort, routine and regular health checks. Easy access to the litter box, warmth and gentle grooming are the basics.

Do senior cats need special food?

Many do, but it depends on the cat's weight and health. Our senior cat diet guide covers the options, and your vet can advise.

— This article is general information, not veterinary advice. If your cat seems unwell, contact a vet.
