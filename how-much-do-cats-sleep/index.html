Cat behaviour

How Much Do
Cats Sleep?

Cats sleep a lot: roughly 12 to 16 hours a day as adults, and more as kittens and seniors. The key is not the exact number but a sudden, lasting change from your own cat's normal pattern.

Sleep needs shift as a cat moves through life. See cat life stages for what each stage looks like, and our cat behaviour guide for related habits.

Typical sleep by age

Life stageTypical sleep per day
Newborn kittens (0 to 6 weeks)About 20 to 22 hours
Kittens (6 weeks to 6 months)About 16 to 20 hours
Adults (1 to 10 years)About 12 to 16 hours
Seniors (10+ years)About 16 to 20 hours

These are typical ranges from one veterinary-reviewed source; other sources give slightly different figures, so treat them as a guide. Your cat's own normal is what counts. See our kitten age chart and when a cat is considered senior to find your cat's stage.

Why cats sleep so much

  • Hunting energy: cats are ambush hunters, with short, intense hunts, so they rest between bursts to conserve energy.
  • Dawn and dusk activity: cats are crepuscular, most active at dawn and dusk, so daytime sleep is normal.
  • Light sleep: about three-quarters of their sleep is light dozing, in many short cycles rather than one long block.
  • Other factors: cold or rainy weather, boredom, age and a full stomach can all increase sleep.

Sleep changes that need attention

The red flag is a sudden, lasting change in how much your cat sleeps, up or down. Watch for:

  • Lethargy: dullness even when awake, loss of interest in food, toys or you, hiding, or missing the litter box.
  • Restlessness: pacing, agitation or night yowling, especially in seniors, which can point to pain or conditions such as an overactive thyroid or high blood pressure.
  • Other symptoms: vomiting, weight loss, changes in thirst or changes in breathing.

Conditions that can change sleep include hyperthyroidism, diabetes, kidney disease, arthritis or other pain, infection, cognitive decline in seniors, anemia and stress. For a cat that wanders or cries at night, also see cat dementia signs.

When to get urgent help

Seek veterinary care promptly if your cat won't wake or respond normally, collapses, has labored breathing, refuses food for more than 24 hours, or strains in the litter box. Find a clinic with our cat vet finder.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours a day do cats sleep?

Adult cats typically sleep about 12 to 16 hours a day, kittens about 16 to 20 hours, and senior cats about 16 to 20 hours.

Do older cats sleep more?

Often, yes, as metabolism and mobility slow. But a sudden, lasting change in sleep, up or down, is the real warning sign.

Are cats nocturnal?

No. Cats are crepuscular, meaning they are most active at dawn and dusk, so daytime sleep is normal.

When is sleeping too much a problem?

If your cat is lethargic, hiding, losing interest in food or toys, or has other symptoms such as vomiting, weight loss or changes in thirst or breathing, see a vet.
