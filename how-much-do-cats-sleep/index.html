Cat behaviour

How Much Do
Cats Sleep?

Cats sleep a lot: roughly 12 to 16 hours a day as adults, and more as kittens and seniors. The exact number matters less than a sudden, lasting change from your own cat's normal pattern, which can be an early sign of pain or illness.

Sleep needs change as a cat moves through life, and a sudden change in sleep is one of the earliest signs that something is wrong. This guide gives the typical hours by age, explains why cats sleep so much, and sets out which changes are worth a call to the vet. For related habits, see our cat behaviour guide.

How many hours do cats sleep?

Most cats sleep about 12 to 16 hours a day, according to PetMD. Kittens may sleep up to 20 hours, while adult cats generally need about 15 hours or less. The numbers below come from WebVet's veterinary-reviewed guide, and other sources give slightly different ranges, so treat them as a guide.

Life stageTypical sleep per dayNotes
Newborn kittens (0 to 6 weeks)About 20 to 22 hoursAlmost all of the day is spent sleeping and feeding
Kittens (6 weeks to 6 months)About 16 to 20 hoursSleep comes in bursts between periods of intense play
Adults (1 to 10 years)About 12 to 16 hoursMost naps are short, and the cat wakes easily
Seniors (10+ years)About 16 to 20 hoursOften more sleep because of health issues or reduced mobility

Why cats sleep so much

  • Saving energy for hunting. Cats are natural predators that conserve energy between bursts of activity. Hunting and exploring use a lot of energy, so cats rest to stay alert and focused.
  • Catnaps keep them ready. PetMD says catnaps typically last 15 to 30 minutes and let a cat rest while still reacting quickly to a threat. WebVet puts about three-quarters of a cat's sleep as light dozing, in many short cycles rather than one long block.
  • Dawn and dusk activity. Cats are crepuscular, meaning they are most active at dawn and dusk. A cat that seems to sleep all day may well be awake at night.
  • Boredom. Lack of mental stimulation can make a cat lethargic and sleepy.
  • Other factors. Breed, diet, lifestyle and age all play a part. WebVet also lists cold or rainy weather and a full stomach.

Normal sleep or a warning sign?

The red flag is a sudden, lasting change from your own cat's pattern, in either direction. Use this table as a quick guide.

Health problems that can change how much a cat sleeps

PetMD lists several illnesses that can increase sleep: hyperthyroidism, diabetes, kidney disease, heart disease, liver disease and cancer. Injuries such as muscle strains or torn ligaments, wounds or infections, and arthritis or joint pain can also lead to more sleep. Stress and anxiety, for example from changes in the household or feeding times, can alter sleep patterns too.

WebVet adds that restlessness, for instance pacing, agitation or night-time yowling, particularly in seniors, can point to pain or conditions such as an overactive thyroid or high blood pressure. A senior cat that wanders or cries at night may also be showing signs of cognitive decline; see cat dementia signs and our guide to why cats meow.

Sleep in senior cats

PetMD notes that senior cats, meaning cats of at least 10, often sleep more, usually because of health issues or reduced mobility. That makes sleep a useful health signal as cats age. A cat that spends more time asleep and less time grooming, jumping or using the litter box may be in pain, even if it is not crying out.

  • Keep a rough note of how much your senior cat sleeps and where. Changes show up faster against a baseline.
  • Make the home easier to move around. Our senior cat home setup guide covers ramps, bedding and access to food and the litter box.
  • See the vet at least every six months once your cat is a senior, and tell them about any change in sleep. Our senior cat health guide lists the warning signs.

Keeping a bored cat active

If your cat seems to sleep because there is nothing else to do, PetMD recommends enrichment to prevent boredom: vertical spaces such as cat trees and shelves, puzzle feeders and regular playtime. Because cats are most active at dawn and dusk, a play session at those times fits their natural pattern. A play session in the evening can also help some cats settle later.

How to track your cat's sleep

  1. Watch for a week. Note roughly when your cat sleeps, where, and how deeply. Does it wake easily?
  2. Record the baseline. Your cat's normal may be well above or below the averages in the table.
  3. Note anything new. Pacing at night, hiding, a change in appetite, missed litter box visits, or less grooming.
  4. Compare after a change. If anything shifts for more than a few days, write down what changed at home, such as a move, a new pet or a new feeding schedule.
  5. Bring your notes to the vet. They make it much easier to work out whether a change is behavioural or medical. Find a clinic with our cat vet finder.

If your cat is awake and noisy at night

Because cats are most active at dawn and dusk, some wake their owners early or stay lively late in the evening. These are practical ideas, not medical advice. A play session before bed, a meal at a consistent time and plenty of daytime activity can help a healthy cat settle. If an older cat is restless, pacing or crying at night, treat it as a possible health signal rather than a habit: see cat dementia signs and our guide to why cats meow, and book a vet visit.

When to get urgent help

Seek veterinary care promptly if your cat will not wake or respond normally, collapses, has laboured breathing, refuses food for more than 24 hours, or strains in the litter box. Loss of appetite or weight loss, and signs of injury or pain such as lameness or stiffness, also need prompt attention.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours a day do cats sleep?

Most cats sleep about 12 to 16 hours a day. Kittens may sleep up to 20 hours, and adult cats generally need about 15 hours or less. Senior cats often sleep more.

Do older cats sleep more?

Often, yes. PetMD says senior cats, meaning those at least 10, often sleep more, usually because of health issues or reduced mobility. A sudden or lasting change is worth mentioning to your vet.

Are cats nocturnal?

No. Cats are crepuscular, meaning they are most active at dawn and dusk. A cat that seems to sleep all day may be awake at night.

How long do cat naps last?

PetMD says catnaps typically last 15 to 30 minutes. WebVet says about three-quarters of a cat's sleep is light dozing.

When is sleeping too much a problem?

If your cat is lethargic, hiding, losing interest in food or toys, or has other changes such as weight loss, vomiting, changes in thirst or breathing, or lameness, contact a vet.

Can boredom make a cat sleep more?

Yes. Lack of mental stimulation can make a cat lethargic and sleepy. Cat trees, shelves, puzzle feeders and regular play can help.

Which illnesses make cats sleep more?

PetMD lists hyperthyroidism, diabetes, kidney disease, heart disease, liver disease and cancer, as well as injuries, infections and arthritis or joint pain. Stress can also change sleep patterns.

— This article is general information, not veterinary advice. If your cat seems unwell, contact a vet.
