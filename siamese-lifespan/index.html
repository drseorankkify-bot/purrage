Siamese Lifespan
& Age Guide.

Siamese cats aren't just known for their striking blue eyes and vocal personalities — they're also one of the longest-living cat breeds out there. If you share your life with one, here's what genuinely affects how long they tend to live, and what's worth watching for as they age.

Average Lifespan

Estimates vary a bit depending on the source, but Siamese cats are consistently reported as living longer than the average domestic cat — commonly cited ranges run from 12 up to 20 years, with many healthy Siamese comfortably reaching the higher end of that range. Part of this comes down to genetics, and part comes down to the fact that Siamese are typically kept as indoor cats, which removes a lot of the risk that shortens outdoor cats' lives. A Siamese named Scooter was once recognized as one of the oldest cats on record, having lived to around 30 — an extreme outlier, but a good illustration of how much genetics and care can extend a healthy cat's life.

What Affects a Siamese Cat's Lifespan

  • Genetics and breeding quality: Cats from responsible breeders tend to have fewer inherited health issues than those from poorly managed breeding lines.
  • Indoor living: Keeping a Siamese indoors removes major risks like traffic, predators, and disease exposure, and is one of the single biggest factors in extending lifespan.
  • Diet and weight management: A high-protein, appropriately portioned diet supports long-term health, and keeping weight in a healthy range matters for any breed.
  • Regular veterinary care: Routine checkups catch the conditions below early, when they're most manageable.

Health Conditions to Watch For

Siamese cats are generally hardy, but there are a handful of conditions this breed is more commonly associated with:

  • Dental and periodontal disease: A significant concern for the breed — regular dental checkups and cleanings make a real difference.
  • Heart conditions: Including congenital heart defects and, in some lines, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) — worth discussing with your vet, especially as your cat ages.
  • Respiratory issues: Some Siamese lines show a predisposition toward respiratory sensitivity.
  • Amyloidosis: A rarer, hereditary protein-related condition documented in Siamese and related breeds, generally not something to worry over day-to-day, but worth your vet being aware of your cat's breed background.

None of this makes a Siamese a fragile cat — quite the opposite, given how long they typically live. It just means knowing what's worth asking your vet about as they move into their mature and senior years.
