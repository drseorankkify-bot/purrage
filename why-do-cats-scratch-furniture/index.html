Cat behaviour

Why Cats
Scratch.

Scratching is normal cat behaviour, not misbehaviour. Cats scratch to mark territory and to condition their claws. The fix is to give your cat something better to scratch, in the right place, rather than to try to stop the behaviour.

This guide covers redirecting scratching at home. See our cat behaviour guide for other habits, and cat body language for stress signals.

Why cats scratch

  • Marking territory: scratching leaves visible claw marks and scent from the foot pads.
  • Claw conditioning: it helps shorten and condition the claws.
  • Comfort: anxious or afraid cats may scratch more, because marking territory is a reassuring and soothing activity for them.

How to redirect scratching

StepWhat to do
1. Provide postsOffer a vertical post tall enough for a full stretch. Try horizontal ones and different shapes as well
2. Vary texturesTry cardboard, fabric or wood planks. If your cat ignores a post, change the texture, shape or orientation before giving up
3. Put it where the scratching isPlace the post directly against the furniture your cat already scratches
4. Move it graduallyOnce your cat uses the post, shift it step by step to its permanent spot
5. Keep it centralChoose a place where the family spends the most time, not an unused corner
6. Make the area invitingAdd hiding spots, toys or elevated perches nearby

What not to do

The sources we read focus on redirection and changes to the environment rather than punishment. Scratching is natural, so the goal is to channel it. If scratching is hard to control, consult a vet.

Scratching and older cats

If an older cat scratches less or stops using a tall post, mention it to your vet, since pain such as arthritis can change how cats move. Horizontal or lower surfaces are worth trying, and the sources suggest offering a mix of shapes. See our senior cat home setup guide. Keeping nails trimmed also helps; see senior cat grooming. Scratching can also increase when a cat is anxious (see body language), so check for changes at home too.

Frequently asked questions

Why do cats scratch furniture?

Scratching is normal behaviour. Cats scratch to mark territory, with visible claw marks and scent from their foot pads, and to shorten and condition their claws.

How do I get my cat to use a scratching post?

Put the post directly against the furniture your cat scratches, then move it gradually to its permanent spot once it is being used. Make it tall enough for a full stretch and try different textures and shapes.

Where should I put a scratching post?

In a central place where the family spends the most time, not an unused corner.

Do anxious cats scratch more?

They can. Marking territory is a reassuring and soothing activity for anxious or afraid cats.

— This article is general information, not veterinary advice. If your cat seems unwell, contact a vet.
