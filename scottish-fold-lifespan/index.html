Scottish Fold Lifespan
& Age Guide.

With their folded ears and round, owlish faces, Scottish Folds are one of the most recognizable breeds around. They're also sweet-natured and generally adaptable companions. If you share your home with one — or you're thinking about it — here's what actually affects how long they tend to live, and what to keep an eye on as they age.

Average Lifespan

Most Scottish Folds live between 11 and 14 years, and well-cared-for cats sometimes live longer still. Estimates vary by source, and the gene responsible for the breed's folded ears is also linked to a joint and bone condition described below, which is the main reason lifespan and comfort can vary more in this breed than in some others. Responsible breeding practices and attentive care both make a real difference here.

Why Scottish Folds Age a Little Differently

The standard veterinary formula for converting to human-equivalent age applies to Scottish Folds the same as any other cat. What sets the breed apart is the cartilage condition tied to its signature folded ears, which can affect joints throughout the body and sometimes becomes more noticeable with age. Because of this, changes in mobility, stiffness, or reluctance to jump are worth taking seriously in this breed rather than dismissing as ordinary aging, and are often the clearest early sign something needs a vet's attention.

What Affects a Scottish Fold's Lifespan

  • Genetics and breeding quality: Responsible breeders avoid pairing two folded-ear cats, since doing so significantly raises the risk of severe joint problems — this single choice has a major effect on quality of life.
  • Indoor living: A calm, home-loving breed generally best suited to indoor life, avoiding traffic, predators, and disease exposure.
  • Weight management: Keeping a Scottish Fold at a healthy weight reduces strain on joints already prone to stiffness.
  • Regular veterinary care: Routine checkups, including joint and ear health, catch the conditions below early, when they're most manageable.

Health Conditions to Watch For

Scottish Folds are generally affectionate and adaptable, but the breed is associated with a few conditions worth knowing about:

  • Osteochondrodysplasia: A cartilage and bone development condition linked directly to the fold gene, which can cause joint stiffness and pain, particularly in the tail, legs, and feet. Severity varies, and responsible breeding significantly reduces risk.
  • Ear infections: The folded ear shape can trap debris and moisture more easily than upright ears, making regular ear checks worthwhile.
  • Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM): A thickening of the heart muscle, seen across many breeds, and worth discussing with your vet as your cat ages.
  • Polycystic Kidney Disease (PKD): Seen occasionally in the breed. Genetic testing is available through many breeders and vets.

None of this makes a Scottish Fold a fragile cat. It simply means knowing what to ask your vet about, particularly as they move into their mature and senior years.
