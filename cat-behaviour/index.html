Cat behaviour

Cat
Behaviour.

Cats communicate through body language, sounds and habits, and most of what they do has a reason. This guide links to our pages on reading body language, purring, meowing, kneading, sleep and scratching, and explains which behaviour changes to take to a vet.

Most cat behaviour has a reason. Scratching marks territory, kneading starts in kittenhood, and purring can mean comfort, a request or stress. This hub links to our guides on each. Because cats often hide illness, a change in behaviour can also be the first sign that something is wrong, which is why age and health come up throughout.

Behaviour changes that deserve a vet visit

PetMD notes that persistent changes in hiding, aggression, energy, appetite, grooming or sociability warrant a vet visit, and that a cat that seems upset for no clear reason may be sick or in pain. In older cats, new meowing, restlessness or confusion can have medical causes such as hyperthyroidism, kidney disease, arthritis or cognitive decline. Find a clinic with our cat vet finder.

Behaviour and age

What is normal changes with life stage. Kittens sleep and play in bursts, adults settle into routines, and seniors can sleep more and may cry or wander at night. See every cat life stage, when a cat is considered senior, and our senior cat care routine. To see how old your cat is in human years, use the PurrAge calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Why do cats behave the way they do?

Most cat behaviour has a purpose: communicating, marking territory, soothing themselves or meeting a need. Reading body language and context shows which.

When is a change in cat behaviour a sign of illness?

Cats often hide illness, so behaviour changes can be the first warning sign. Persistent changes in hiding, aggression, energy, appetite, grooming or sociability warrant a vet visit.

Do cats behave differently as they get older?

Yes. Older cats may sleep more, meow or cry more, or become confused, and these changes can have medical causes, so a vet check is the first step.

Is it okay to punish a cat for unwanted behaviour?

The sources we read advise redirecting behaviour and meeting the cat's needs rather than punishing it. Shouting or spraying water can damage trust.

— This article is general information, not veterinary advice. If your cat seems unwell, contact a vet.
