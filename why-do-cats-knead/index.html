Cat behaviour

Why Do Cats
Knead?

Kneading, the rhythmic pushing of the paws, begins in kittenhood when kittens knead their mother to stimulate milk flow. Some cats carry the habit into adulthood. No one knows exactly why, but there are three main theories.

Kneading is one part of how cats behave and communicate. See our cat behaviour guide for more, and cat life stages to see where kittenhood fits.

Where kneading comes from

Kneading starts with nursing. Kittens knead around their mother's teat to stimulate the flow of milk, and are rewarded with milk. Some cats keep the behaviour into adulthood, when it no longer serves that purpose.

Why adult cats still knead

Cats Protection says no one knows for certain why some adult cats keep kneading, and offers three theories:

TheoryThe ideaHow well supported
It feels goodKneading may release relaxing chemicals in the brain, so it is rewardingAssumed, not confirmed
It is automaticCertain textures under the paws may trigger kneading as a hard-wired responseOne of the main ideas
It is inheritedWildcats may have patted down grass to make a sleeping spotLess well supported

Because cats rarely do something for no reason, adult kneading probably has a purpose even if we cannot see it. The feel-good theory suggests it may go with contentment, but there is no single confirmed meaning.

If kneading hurts

  • Keep claws trimmed. Short claws make kneading much more comfortable. Our senior cat grooming guide covers nail care, which matters more as claws thicken with age.
  • Put a blanket or cushion on your lap as a barrier between claws and skin.
  • Offer a soft bed your cat can knead instead. Cats also tend to prefer beds off the ground.
  • Don't try to stop it. Kneading is a natural behaviour, so manage the claws rather than the habit.

When to ask a vet

Kneading itself is normal. A sudden change in behaviour is the thing to watch, for example a cat that becomes unsettled or shows other changes in appetite, grooming or energy. Persistent changes warrant a vet visit; see our cat vet finder and cat body language for other signals to watch.

Frequently asked questions

Why do cats knead blankets and people?

Kneading begins in kittenhood, when kittens knead their mother to stimulate milk flow. Adult cats may continue because it feels good, because certain textures trigger it automatically, or from inherited instinct. No one knows for certain.

Does kneading mean a cat is happy?

It may. One theory is that kneading releases relaxing chemicals in the brain, which would fit with contentment, but this is assumed rather than confirmed.

How do I stop a cat's claws hurting when it kneads?

Keep the claws trimmed and put a blanket or cushion on your lap as a barrier.

Should I be worried if my cat kneads a lot?

Kneading is normal. Worry is warranted only if it comes with other changes in behaviour, appetite or energy, in which case contact a vet.

— This article is general information, not veterinary advice. If your cat seems unwell, contact a vet.
