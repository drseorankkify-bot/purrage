Devon Rex Lifespan
& Age Guide.

With their oversized ears, impish face, and soft, curly coat, Devon Rex cats look like no other breed — and they're just as playful and people-oriented as they look. If you share your home with one — or you're thinking about it — here's what actually affects how long they tend to live, and what to keep an eye on as they age.

Average Lifespan

Most Devon Rex cats live between 9 and 15 years. Estimates vary by source, and the breed's relatively small founding gene pool means a handful of inherited conditions carry more weight than in more genetically diverse breeds. Good screening from a responsible breeder, along with routine care for their unusual coat and skin, both help support a longer life.

Why Devon Rex Cats Age a Little Differently

The standard veterinary formula for converting to human-equivalent age applies to Devon Rex cats the same as any other cat. What sets the breed apart is its fine, sparse coat, which offers less insulation and sun protection than a typical cat's fur, and its naturally high energy, which many Devon Rex keep well into their senior years. Because of this, a real drop in their usual mischief and climbing, rather than general stillness, is often the clearer sign something is worth mentioning to your vet.

What Affects a Devon Rex's Lifespan

  • Genetics and breeding quality: Responsible breeders screen for inherited heart and muscle conditions, which meaningfully lowers long-term health risk in this breed.
  • Indoor living with climate control: Their thin coat makes Devon Rex cats sensitive to cold, so a warm, sheltered indoor life suits them better than outdoor exposure.
  • Skin and coat care: Their coat produces oil that a full fur coat would normally absorb, so some Devon Rex benefit from occasional bathing and regular skin checks.
  • Regular veterinary care: Routine checkups, including heart screening as they age, catch the conditions below early, when they're most manageable.

Health Conditions to Watch For

Devon Rex cats are playful and generally resilient, but like most purebred lines, they're associated with a few conditions worth knowing about:

  • Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM): A thickening of the heart muscle and one of the more significant health concerns in the breed. Regular heart screening is used by many reputable breeders and vets.
  • Hereditary myopathy: A muscle condition that can cause weakness or spasticity, more often seen in this breed than in cats generally.
  • Patellar luxation: A kneecap condition that can affect mobility, worth mentioning to your vet if you notice occasional limping or an unusual gait.
  • Skin conditions: Their fine coat and active oil production can lead to skin irritation, which regular gentle care helps manage.

None of this makes a Devon Rex a fragile cat. It simply means knowing what to ask your vet about, particularly as they move into their mature and senior years.
