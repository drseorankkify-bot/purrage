The quick answer

Cat years to
human years.

A 5-year-old cat is about 36 in human years, and a 10-year-old cat is about 56. The usual conversion counts a cat's first year as about 15 human years, the second as another 9, and each year after that as about 4.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert cat years to human years?

The first year is about 15 human years, the second adds about 9 (24 at age 2), and each year after adds about 4. A 6-year-old cat is about 40.

How old is a 10-year-old cat in human years?

About 56. Ten is also the age at which vets generally begin to treat a cat as a senior.

Is the “multiply by 7” rule accurate?

No. Cats mature far faster than that in their first two years and age more slowly afterwards, so one multiplier gets both ends of life wrong.

How do I convert human years to cat years?

For a human age above 24, subtract 24, divide by 4 and add 2. For example, 40 human years is (40 − 24) ÷ 4 + 2 = 6 cat years. The calculator does this when you switch to Human → Cat.
