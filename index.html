The quick answer

Cat years to
human years.

A 5-year-old cat is about 36 in human years, and a 10-year-old cat is about 56. The usual conversion counts a cat's first year as about 15 human years, the second year as another 9, and every year after that as about 4. The calculator above uses this rule, and the chart below lists every age from 1 to 25.

Why “multiply by 7” does not work

The old rule of thumb multiplies a pet's age by seven. For cats, that makes a 1-year-old a 7-year-old child, when a cat of that age is closer to a teenager. The multiplier then overstates age in later life. Cats grow quickly in their first two years and slow down afterwards, which is why the conversion above is front-loaded. Read the full explanation in how do cats age, or see our worked examples in how old is my cat in human years.

How to convert human years to cat years

Reverse the rule. For a human age above 24, subtract 24, divide by 4 and add 2. A 40-year-old person is about (40 − 24) ÷ 4 + 2 = 6 in cat years. Below 24, a human age of 15 is about 1 cat year, and 24 is about 2. The Human → Cat toggle in the calculator does this for you.

What your cat's age means for care

The number matters mostly because it tells you what to look out for. Vets generally treat a cat as a senior from about 10, when checkups, weight, teeth and joints deserve closer attention. The stage cards above show where your cat fits, and these guides go further:

Don't know your cat's age?

If you adopted a cat with no birth date, a vet can estimate age from the teeth, coat, eyes and body condition. Our guide to how to tell how old a cat is explains what each sign suggests. Once you have an estimate, enter it in the calculator and see the life stage.

How long do cats live?

The conversion tells you how old your cat is in human terms. How long a cat is likely to live is a separate question. In an English study of 4,009 cats, the median age at death was 14.0 years, and neutering, weight, breed and outdoor access all affected it. See how long do cats live for the evidence, the longest-living cat breeds, and the oldest cat ever.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert cat years to human years?

A cat's first year is about 15 human years, the second year adds about 9 (so 24 at age 2), and every year after that adds about 4. A 6-year-old cat is about 40 in human years.

How old is a 10-year-old cat in human years?

About 56. Ten years is also the point where vets generally begin to treat a cat as a senior.

Is the “multiply by 7” rule accurate?

No. Cats mature far faster than that in their first two years, then age more slowly, so one fixed multiplier gets both ends of life wrong.

How do I convert human years to cat years?

For a human age above 24, subtract 24, divide by 4, then add 2. For example, 40 human years is (40 − 24) ÷ 4 + 2 = 6 cat years. The calculator above does this when you switch to Human → Cat.

At what age is a cat a senior?

Vets generally count a cat as a senior from about 10 years old. Our guide to when a cat is senior explains the stages and what changes.

How long do cats live?

In an English study of 4,009 cats, the median age at death was 14.0 years. See our lifespan guide for how neutering, weight, breed and outdoor access change the number.
