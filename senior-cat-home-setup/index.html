Senior cat care

Senior Cat
Home Setup.

A senior cat home setup is about removing small obstacles: a litter box that is easy to enter, a ramp to the favourite sofa, a warm bed away from drafts, and food and water close by. These changes are cheap, and they matter because joint pain and slower movement are very common in older cats.

PetMD estimates that more than 90% of cats over 12 have some form of arthritis, often without obvious signs. Use this room-by-room checklist as part of your senior cat care routine, and read our guide to arthritis signs in cats if your cat seems stiff.

Room-by-room checklist

Litter box

PetMD says senior cats should have a box on the floor where they spend most of their time, and that wider, uncovered boxes with lower sides or entryways are easier than high-sided or covered ones. Our litter box guide explains sizes, litter and cleaning.

Food and water

  • PetMD suggests several bowls or water fountains in easy-to-reach places, and food and water on every floor in a multi-level home.
  • Some cats prefer slightly raised bowls.
  • In a multi-cat home, make sure younger cats are not crowding an older cat away from meals.

For what to put in the bowls, read our senior cat diet guide.

Beds and warmth

PetMD notes that older cats can find it harder to regulate body temperature, so offer draft-free sleeping spots in warm rooms and consider a heated cat bed or pad in cooler rooms. Use products made for pets and follow the maker's instructions. Put beds in quiet places the cat already likes rather than moving them somewhere new.

Ramps, steps and floors

  • Add a ramp or steps to bed, sofa or window perch. PetMD says to choose one that is stable under your cat's weight and, ideally, carpeted for grip.
  • Put rugs or yoga mats on slippery flooring so your cat can walk and jump with confidence.
  • Avoid rearranging furniture often, so the cat can adapt to the new equipment and keep a familiar map of the home.

Light, safety and routine

  • PetMD notes that night vision declines with age, so night-lights can help your cat find their way in the dark. VCA also suggests extra lighting if sight is fading.
  • Limit access to balconies, ledges and high shelves that create fall risks.
  • Keep routines steady. Feed at the same times each day, and avoid sudden changes.
  • If your cat is stressed, PetMD says calming pheromones may help in mild cases. Talk to your vet if the stress is serious.

Check how it is working

For a few weeks, watch whether your cat uses the new ramp, bowls and bed. If your cat still hesitates to jump, misses the box or sleeps in new spots, mention it to your vet. Pain can be hard to see, and our senior cat health guide explains what to bring to a checkup. If your cat is also confused or restless at night, read about cat dementia signs. Grooming gets harder with age too; see senior cat grooming.

Frequently asked questions

How can I make my home easier for an older cat?

Start with the litter box and ramps: a low-entry box on the main floor, steps to favourite spots, rugs on slippery floors, and bowls and beds close together.

Do senior cats need heated beds?

PetMD says older cats can struggle to regulate their temperature, and calls heated beds and pads a good addition for cooler rooms. Use a pet-safe product and follow the instructions.

Are ramps better than stairs for senior cats?

Either works if it is stable and has good grip. PetMD suggests a carpeted ramp that holds your cat's weight steadily.

Should I move my senior cat's food and water bowls?

Only to make them easier to reach. Offer several bowls or a fountain, and put food and water on every floor your cat uses.

Why does my old cat avoid jumping?

Joint pain is a common reason. Ramps help, and a vet can check for arthritis and discuss pain relief.

— This article is general information, not veterinary advice. If your cat seems unwell or in pain, contact a vet.
