Breed guide

Domestic Shorthair
Lifespan & Age Guide.

Not a formal breed but the most common type of cat around, Domestic Shorthairs come in every color and pattern imaginable, with a wide genetic mix behind them. That mix tends to work in their favor. If you share your home with one — or you're thinking about it — here's what actually affects how long they tend to live, and what to keep an eye on as they age.

Average Lifespan

Most Domestic Shorthairs live between 12 and 18 years, and many well-cared-for indoor cats live into their late teens or even early twenties. Estimates vary by source, but this group tends to be among the longer-lived and lowest-risk of all cats, mainly because their broad genetic diversity means no single inherited condition is concentrated the way it can be in a narrow purebred line. Indoor living and routine care make the biggest difference to how long an individual cat lives.

Why Domestic Shorthairs Age a Little Differently

The standard veterinary formula for converting to human-equivalent age applies to Domestic Shorthairs the same as any other cat. What sets this group apart is simply how varied it is — build, coat, temperament, and health risks differ far more cat to cat than within a single breed, so general guidance matters more here than breed-specific patterns. Without a consistent breed profile to compare against, changes in your own cat's normal habits, rather than how they stack up to "the breed," are the most reliable signal of aging.

What Affects a Domestic Shorthair's Lifespan

  • Genetic diversity: A wide, unconcentrated gene pool generally means fewer inherited conditions clustering in any one cat.
  • Indoor living: Indoor Domestic Shorthairs consistently live significantly longer than those with regular outdoor access, avoiding traffic, predators, and disease exposure.
  • Weight management: As with any cat, staying at a healthy weight protects the heart, joints, and long-term health.
  • Regular veterinary care: Routine checkups catch common age-related conditions early, when they're most manageable.

Health Conditions to Watch For

Domestic Shorthairs don't carry the breed-specific conditions purebred cats often do, but a few general conditions are still worth knowing about:

  • Obesity: One of the most common and most preventable health issues in cats generally, with real effects on joints, heart, and diabetes risk.
  • Dental disease: Common in cats of all kinds, and worth staying ahead of with regular dental checkups.
  • Chronic kidney disease: One of the more common conditions in older cats generally, making regular senior checkups worthwhile.
  • Heart disease: Including hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM), which can occur in any cat and is worth discussing with your vet as your cat ages.

None of this makes a Domestic Shorthair a fragile cat — quite the opposite. It simply means knowing what to ask your vet about, particularly as they move into their mature and senior years.
