Breed guide

American Shorthair
Lifespan & Age Guide.

Easygoing, sturdy, and famously unfussy, American Shorthairs are one of the most reliably healthy purebred cats around. They're also one of the longer-lived breeds. If you share your home with one — or you're thinking about it — here's what actually affects how long they tend to live, and what to keep an eye on as they age.

Average Lifespan

Most American Shorthairs live between 15 and 20 years, placing them among the longer-lived purebred cats. Estimates vary by source, but the breed is widely considered sturdy and low-risk, with a broader gene pool and fewer breed-specific inherited conditions than many other purebred lines. Good weight management and regular vet care go a long way toward reaching the upper end of that range.

Why American Shorthairs Age a Little Differently

The standard veterinary formula for converting to human-equivalent age applies to American Shorthairs the same as any other cat. What sets the breed apart is less about health risk and more about how little tends to go wrong — their robust build and calm, adaptable temperament mean many American Shorthairs show few visible signs of aging until relatively late, so routine checkups matter more than watching for obvious changes at home.

What Affects an American Shorthair's Lifespan

  • Genetics and breeding quality: The breed's broad gene pool means responsible breeding mainly supports an already strong baseline rather than screening out widespread problems.
  • Indoor living: American Shorthairs are adaptable but still benefit from a secure indoor life, avoiding traffic, predators, and disease exposure.
  • Weight management: A sturdy, medium-sized build can still gain weight easily if underexercised. Keeping them lean protects the heart and joints.
  • Regular veterinary care: Routine checkups help this already hardy breed stay that way well into old age.

Health Conditions to Watch For

American Shorthairs are known for being a particularly healthy breed, but a few conditions are still worth knowing about:

  • Obesity: Not a disease on its own, but the most common health risk in the breed, given their sturdy build and hearty appetite.
  • Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM): A thickening of the heart muscle, seen across many breeds, and worth discussing with your vet as your cat ages.
  • Dental disease: Common in cats of all breeds, and worth staying ahead of with regular dental checkups.
  • Kidney disease: More common in older cats generally, making regular senior checkups worthwhile.

None of this makes an American Shorthair a fragile cat — quite the opposite. It simply means knowing what to ask your vet about, particularly as they move into their mature and senior years.
