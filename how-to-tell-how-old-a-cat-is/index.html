Age guide

How to Tell How
Old a Cat Is.

If you adopted a stray or a shelter cat with no records, you may not know its birthday. You can still get a useful estimate by looking at its teeth, eyes, coat, body condition and movement. This guide explains each clue and how far you can trust it.

Be realistic about accuracy. Vets can place a kitten within a few weeks, but for adult cats they usually give a range, such as "young adult" or "senior." Health, diet and genetics all change how old a cat looks.

1. Teeth

Teeth are the most useful clue, especially in young cats.

  • Kittens: baby teeth start to appear at around three weeks, and a full set of baby teeth (26) is in by about six weeks. Adult teeth (30) arrive between roughly three and six months.
  • Young adults: clean, bright teeth with little or no tartar.
  • Mature cats: tartar builds up and gums may redden.
  • Seniors: more visible wear, heavier tartar, gum disease and sometimes missing teeth.

Dental health depends on diet and care as well as age, so a cat with great teeth could be older than it looks, and a young cat with poor dental care can look older.

2. Eyes

Young cats typically have bright, clear eyes with no discharge. As cats age, the lens can take on a slightly cloudy, bluish look called nuclear sclerosis, which is a normal ageing change. Senior cats can also develop cataracts, which are more serious and need a vet's attention. Changes to the colour of the iris can occur with age too.

3. Coat

Kittens and young adults usually have a soft, smooth, tidy coat. Older cats groom less, so the coat can feel rougher or thinner and mat more easily, especially along the back. Coat quality is also affected by diet and illness, so use it as a hint only.

4. Muscle tone and weight

Young adult cats usually carry the most lean muscle. Middle-aged cats often gain weight, while seniors are more likely to lose muscle along the spine and hips and may look bony even if they eat well. Sudden weight loss at any age is a reason to see a vet.

5. Movement and energy

Young cats leap and play freely. Older cats may hesitate before jumping, take stairs more slowly, sleep more, or show a stiff gait. Arthritis is common in older cats and often goes unnoticed because cats hide discomfort.

6. Behaviour changes

Seniors can be more vocal, clingier, or less tolerant of stress, and some change their sleeping patterns or litter box habits. These signs overlap with health problems, so they are a reason to talk to a vet, not a way to date your cat.

Can a vet tell exactly?

A vet checks teeth, eyes, coat, body condition, joints and sometimes bloodwork, then gives an estimated age or age range. Some shelters and vets also use microchip records or history from previous owners. If you want a number to use, our PurrAge calculator turns any estimated age into human years.

Frequently asked questions

Can you tell a cat's age from its teeth?

Yes, roughly. Tooth development is very reliable for kittens, and wear and tartar give an adult-age range, but diet and dental care affect how teeth look.

How old is a cat with a lot of tartar?

Heavy tartar suggests a mature or senior cat, but it can also appear in younger cats that have not had dental care. Have a vet check it.

Do cats' eyes change colour with age?

Kittens' blue eyes change to their adult colour within the first couple of months. Later changes, such as a cloudy look, are usually age-related and worth mentioning to your vet.

How old is my rescue cat?

A vet can give you an estimated range. Use that range for feeding, vet visits and care, then see how old your cat is in human years.
