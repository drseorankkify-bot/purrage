Senior cat care

Senior Cat Not Using
the Litter Box.

When an older cat starts missing the litter box, the cause is usually pain, illness or a box that has become hard to use, not bad behaviour. The first step is a vet visit, because several common senior conditions show up this way. Once the vet has checked, small changes to the box and its location often help.

This guide covers why it happens, how to tell the likely causes apart, and what to change at home. It is part of our senior cat care guide.

Why senior cats stop using the litter box

VCA Animal Hospitals lists several medical causes of cats going outside the box. Many of them are more common in older cats:

Kidney disease or diabetesMore urine than usual, so the box fills faster and the cat looks elsewhereKidney disease signs

Several of these can happen together, which is why the exam comes first. VCA says the first step for any cat going outside the box is a veterinary exam, which typically includes a urine test and a stool check.

See the vet first

Book a visit if your cat has more than one or two accidents, or if the change is sudden. Take notes with you: when it started, whether it is urine or stool, where it happens, and any changes in drinking, appetite or weight. Our cat vet finder can help you find a clinic.

Go to a vet the same day if your cat is straining to pass urine and little or nothing comes out, is crying in the box, or seems unwell. A cat that cannot pass urine is an emergency, and this is especially serious in male cats.

Make the litter box easier to use

Once your vet has ruled out or begun treating a medical cause, adjust the setup. These points come from VCA and PetMD:

  • Use a low-entry box. PetMD recommends wider, uncovered boxes with lower sides or entryways over high-sided or covered ones. VCA suggests that a cat who struggles to get in may do better with a low container, such as an under-bed storage box.
  • Choose a big box. VCA notes that cats usually prefer a box about 1.5 times their body length, measured from the nose to the base of the tail.
  • Put one on the main living floor. PetMD says a senior cat should have a box on the floor where they spend most of their time. VCA advises at least two locations, and a box on each floor in large homes.
  • Keep it easy to reach. Avoid stairs, tight corners and noisy places such as next to the washing machine.
  • Scoop every day. VCA recommends scooping at least once a day, replacing clumping litter with fresh litter monthly, and washing the box with mild soap. Do not use bleach, ammonia or strong chemicals.
  • Watch what is in the box. PetMD notes that checking more often helps you spot constipation or diarrhoea early.

Test one change at a time

VCA's advice is to change one feature at a time, such as litter type, depth or box style, and give each change one to two weeks while you keep a short diary. Most cats seem to prefer clumping clay litter, and depth can matter too, so it is worth comparing a deeper and a shallower layer.

Accident or marking?

VCA separates two things. Marking is a form of communication, more often involves urine, and is usually linked to anxiety or tension with another cat or person. Toileting outside the box is about emptying the bladder or bowels. It can be a preference for a particular spot, or a dislike of the box itself caused by litter, box design, pain or fear while using it. In an older cat, pain or illness is far more likely than marking, but if no medical cause is found, VCA suggests asking for a behavioural assessment.

Do not punish accidents

A cat that avoids the box is usually reacting to discomfort. Punishment can add fear and make the problem worse. Stay calm, keep the cat's routine steady and work with your vet.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my old cat peeing outside the litter box?

Common reasons are sore joints that make the box hard to use, kidney disease or diabetes that raise urine output, a urinary tract problem, or confusion. A vet exam and urine test are the first step.

Is it normal for senior cats to have accidents?

Occasional changes can happen as cats age, but regular accidents are a signal to look for a cause, not a normal part of ageing.

What kind of litter box is best for an older cat?

A large, uncovered box with a low entry, placed on the floor where the cat spends most of the day, suits most senior cats. Test one change at a time.

How many litter boxes does a senior cat need?

VCA advises offering boxes in at least two locations, and one on each floor in a large home. A senior cat should have one on their main living floor.

When is it an emergency?

If your cat strains to urinate and produces little or nothing, cries in the box, or seems unwell, see a vet the same day.

— This article is general information, not veterinary advice. If your cat seems unwell or cannot pass urine, contact a vet straight away.
