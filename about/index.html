The old myth

Why We Built PurrAge

Most "cat years" calculators still use the old rule everyone grew up with — one cat year equals seven human years. It's catchy, it's memorable, and it's wrong. Cats don't age at a steady rate: they mature explosively in their first two years, then settle into a slower, steadier pace for the rest of their lives.

PurrAge uses the veterinary-recognized formula instead — the same age curve vets actually reference — so the number you get back means something real, not just a fun fact.

More than a number

What Makes This Different

A number alone doesn't tell you much. That's why every result on PurrAge is tied to one of six life stages — kitten, junior, prime, mature, senior, and geriatric — each with a short, honest note on what tends to change at that point in a cat's life. It's meant to be a starting point for paying closer attention, not a replacement for actual veterinary guidance.

Small and focused

Our Approach

PurrAge is a small, focused tool. We're not trying to be everything for every pet — just genuinely good at the one thing we do. As the site grows, we'll be adding breed-specific guides and deeper life-stage content, always grounded in real research rather than guesswork.
