Cat behaviour

Why Do Cats
Meow So Much?

Most meowing is normal. Cats meow to greet you, to ask for food or attention, or to get through a door. But a cat that is meowing more than usual, especially an older cat, needs a vet check first, because pain, thyroid disease, kidney disease and confusion can all cause it.

This guide covers everyday meowing and the age-related causes. If your older cat cries mostly at night, also read our guide to cat dementia signs.

Meowing in older cats

If an older cat suddenly starts meowing more, see a vet before assuming it is a habit. Overactive thyroid and kidney disease become more likely with age and can both cause excess hunger, thirst, restlessness or irritability. Hyperthyroidism is more common in cats over 7, with symptoms that include weight loss, increased appetite and thirst, more urination, vomiting, diarrhoea, restlessness and greasy or matted fur. Read more in our guides to hyperthyroidism and kidney disease.

Older cats can also become confused as part of age-related decline, which Save-a-Cat describes as the cat version of Alzheimer's disease. Disoriented crying, often at night, is typical. Loss of vision and hearing can also be confusing and distressing for an older cat.

What to do

  1. See a vet first if the meowing is excessive, has started suddenly, or your cat is over 7. Find one with our cat vet finder.
  2. Check the basics: an empty water bowl, a dirty litter box, or a cat shut in a room can all prompt crying.
  3. Keep a log of when your cat meows, to spot patterns and triggers.
  4. Respond to quiet, not to noise. Give attention when your cat is calm, not while it is demanding.
  5. Add enrichment: daily play and puzzle or foraging toys help a bored or lonely cat.
  6. For night crying in older cats, try night lights in dark areas, leave the bedroom door slightly open, and add an evening play session. A vet can advise on treatment for cognitive symptoms. See also our senior cat home setup guide.

What not to do

Do not shout at, hit or spray a meowing cat. It will not stop the meowing and can damage your cat's trust in you. Also avoid ignoring the meowing completely before you have checked that your cat does not need something.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my cat meowing so much all of a sudden?

A sudden increase in meowing can be caused by pain, illness or age-related confusion. Any cat meowing more than usual should be checked by a vet, especially a cat over 7.

Why do older cats meow at night?

Older cats can become disoriented from age-related cognitive decline and cry, often at night. Overactive thyroid, kidney disease and pain can also cause restlessness and meowing.

Should I ignore my cat when it meows?

Not before checking that its basic needs are met and that it is well. For attention-seeking, give attention when your cat is quiet rather than while it meows.

Will neutering stop a cat meowing?

Spaying or neutering is the best way to prevent the yowling that goes with mating behaviour.

— This article is general information, not veterinary advice. If your cat seems unwell, contact a vet.
